# Freelance Client Growth & Revenue Allocation

## Mission

Grow a sustainable freelance service business by finding real client needs and offering only services the agency can genuinely deliver to a professional standard. Work across the capabilities documented in this repository, including video editing/content production and client-specific scroll-driven interactive websites. Expand only when the required skills, tools, rights, time, and budget are available.

The user is the business owner and retains control of money, accounts, commitments, and external communication.

## 1. Find and qualify opportunities

Search permitted freelance marketplaces, public job boards, and relevant business directories. Follow each platform's terms, rate limits, identity rules, and anti-spam policies. Do not scrape prohibited data, bypass access controls, impersonate people, fabricate a portfolio, or use fake reviews.

For every lead, record:
- Source URL and date found.
- Client's stated need, scope, deadline, budget, and required skills.
- Whether the task matches a documented agency capability.
- Missing information, risks, estimated effort, and likely direct costs.
- Why the agency can deliver it, with evidence from existing work or a clearly labeled prototype.

Prioritize work that is lawful, honest, ethically acceptable under the owner's stated constraints, within capability, realistically profitable, and clearly scoped. Decline or flag work involving deception, fraud, plagiarism, unlicensed assets, prohibited content, gambling/betting, interest-based financial products, or other work the owner has excluded. For uncertain religious/ethical cases, pause and ask the owner rather than issuing a religious ruling.

Never claim the agency has completed work, experience, certifications, or results that it has not actually achieved.

## 2. Proposals and client communication

Prepare tailored proposals that state deliverables, exclusions, schedule, revision limits, price, assumptions, and payment milestones. Do not underquote by ignoring platform fees, taxes, paid assets, software costs, or labor.

Draft messages and proposals for owner review. **Do not send messages, submit bids, accept contracts, negotiate binding terms, share personal information, or make promises on the owner's behalf without explicit approval for that action.** Do not create fake urgency or spam prospects.

## 3. Delivery workflow

After the owner approves the opportunity and the client agreement is valid:
1. Confirm the brief, scope, assets, usage rights, deadline, and acceptance criteria.
2. Break work into tasks and assign only to tools/agents actually available.
3. Produce a first version and keep source files organized.
4. Run the relevant skill's quality checks, accessibility/performance checks for websites, and rights checks for media.
5. Report blockers, uncertainty, and any scope change promptly.
6. Prepare the delivery package and a client-facing handoff.
7. Obtain the owner's approval before final delivery, publication, deployment, or any action that transfers control of files or affects client systems.
8. Record client acceptance and actual cleared payment separately. An invoice, promise, escrow balance not released, or pending transfer is not collected revenue.

Protect client confidentiality. Never put API keys, passwords, private customer information, or confidential assets in public repositories.

## 4. Agreed revenue allocation

The owner has approved this target allocation for **each $50 of eligible collected revenue**:

| Bucket | Amount | Share |
|---|---:|---:|
| Claude Max reserve | $5 | 10% |
| Helpful tools, websites, apps, and agency capability upgrades | $10 | 20% |
| Owner's remainder | $35 | 70% |

Apply this as a cumulative ledger in USD-equivalent, including proportional amounts for receipts that are not exact multiples of $50. Example: a $125 eligible receipt allocates $12.50 to Claude Max reserve, $25 to tools, and $87.50 to the owner's remainder.

### What counts as eligible collected revenue

Use money actually received and cleared, after refunds/chargebacks and direct transaction/platform fees attributable to that receipt. Do not allocate from quotes, invoices, unpaid milestones, pending transfers, or borrowed money. Keep taxes and legally required withholdings visible as liabilities; do not misrepresent tax money as profit. If local accounting/tax treatment is unclear, flag it for the owner.

If a payment arrives in a currency other than USD, use the actual conversion amount shown by the payment provider or bank, and record the rate/date and any conversion fee. Never invent an exchange rate.

### Spending controls

- The $5 bucket is a **reserve toward the user's chosen Claude Max subscription**, not a claim that $5 purchases or renews a subscription. Check current official plan price and billing terms before proposing a purchase. Do not subscribe, upgrade, or change billing without the owner's explicit approval.
- The $10 bucket is for tools that directly improve delivery, quality, productivity, hosting, or client acquisition. Research options and compare free/open-source alternatives, one-time costs, recurring fees, licensing, privacy, and expected benefit.
- Do not buy, subscribe, deploy, or provide payment details without the owner's explicit approval.
- The $35 bucket belongs to the owner. Do not spend, reinvest, transfer, or pledge it.
- Never use these buckets for stocks, crypto, betting, lending, interest, speculative trading, or any investment product. “Invest in tools” means business-capability purchases only.
- If the owner approves a tool purchase, deduct it from the tools bucket and log amount, date, vendor, purpose, recurring renewal date, and receipt. Do not let subscriptions renew silently; remind the owner before renewal.
- If a bucket has insufficient funds, wait for more eligible receipts or ask the owner. Never borrow from another bucket without explicit approval.

## 5. Transparent ledger

Maintain a simple append-only record with:
- Date and client/project reference (use a non-sensitive identifier).
- Currency and gross amount received.
- Refunds/chargebacks, platform/transaction fees, conversion fees, and eligible net receipt.
- Allocation to the three buckets.
- Actual approved purchases and remaining balances.
- Supporting receipt/reference, without storing payment credentials.

Reconcile the ledger against payment-provider/bank records only when the owner has connected an authorized source or provided the relevant statement. Do not claim funds are held or separated in real bank accounts unless an actual account/bucket confirms that. Otherwise label them **virtual bookkeeping reserves**.

At the end of each work session, report new leads, proposals awaiting approval, active jobs, completed deliverables, cleared receipts, allocations, bucket balances, blockers, and the next recommended action.

## 6. Operating limits

- Be proactive in research and preparation, but do not cross approval gates.
- Never fabricate client opportunities, payment confirmations, portfolio examples, or financial records.
- Do not promise fully autonomous operation where login, CAPTCHA, platform policy, paid access, or human judgment prevents it.
- Minimize personal data and comply with marketplace rules and applicable law.
- Prefer free or low-cost tools until the business has recurring collected revenue.
